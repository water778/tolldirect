Dear EZDriveMA Commercial Department,

Please route this note to the MassDOT official responsible for EZDriveMA commercial accounts and the Transportation Network Company toll procedure under M.G.L. c. 159A½. This is a request for a discussion only. It is not an application, a procurement response, or a request to implement anything.

TollDirect does not request Uber, Lyft, MassDOT, or the driver to finance passenger tolls. The passenger is the sole source of payment.

When a rideshare vehicle passes a toll gantry during an active passenger trip, the proposed system would automatically direct that passenger’s toll payment to the E-ZPass MA account associated with the vehicle. It would use the payment method already authorized for the trip. No additional passenger action, application, or duplicate charge would be required. The driver would not advance the toll, and neither Uber nor Lyft would advance it.

The central question is whether this passenger-funded, trip-linked settlement can be implemented without first debiting the driver’s existing E-ZPass balance.

Today, the gantry read is posted to the driver’s E-ZPass MA account, and the driver must already have funded that account. The passenger later pays the toll inside the trip fare, and the transportation network company reimburses the driver. We are not asking how to redirect that reimbursement. We are asking whether MassDOT could discuss a path in which the official toll is matched to the active trip and credited from the passenger’s existing trip payment, so the driver’s prepaid balance is never drawn for that passenger toll.

We understand the current published process. Account holders replenish their own accounts. Your terms restrict assignment without written consent. Official posts are delayed: a Mass.gov sample shows an exit on 21 September and a post on 24 September, and a 2018 MassDOT report described a two-to-five-day trip-building period. A balance at or below $0.00 can produce a Pay By Plate invoice. The rideshare commercial-rate adjustment can take up to two weeks. We are not asking to change that process now.

We would like clarification on the following:

1. Can passenger-funded, trip-linked settlement be implemented without first debiting the driver’s existing E-ZPass MA balance?
2. If the official post is delayed by several days, can an authorized credit be applied before or with that post, so the passenger toll never draws the driver’s balance?
3. What happens to an insufficient or zero balance during that delay? Is there any confirmed period before a Pay By Plate invoice, a higher rate, or a penalty is issued? We have not found a 24-hour or 48-hour grace period.
4. How would payment processing work: which office would receive a trip-linked credit, what payment file or interface would be required, and what confirmation would be returned?
5. What technical integration would MassDOT require from the transportation network company and from any licensed payment provider, and is any such interface available today?
6. How would a later commercial-rate adjustment, a refund, a failed credit, or a duplicate crossing be handled so the passenger is charged once and the driver is neither debited nor reimbursed for the same toll?
7. Is there an existing institutional, fleet, or dedicated settlement account that could support this, or would a new written arrangement be required? Which office would decide, and what contract path would apply?
8. Would MassDOT be willing to review a paper pilot limited to one approved TNC, E-ZPass MA accounts, and Massachusetts facilities, with no live payments until written approval?

No account numbers, credentials, or funds are included. Please direct us to the correct office if this is not it.
